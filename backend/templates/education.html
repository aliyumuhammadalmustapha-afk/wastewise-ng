Everything you need to know about waste in Nigeria

Did you know?

Nigeria generates 32 million tonnes of solid waste annually – less than 40% is properly managed.

Source: World Bank Solid Waste Management Review

Moderate

Plastic

Pure water sachet PET bottles Nylon bags Ghana-must-go

Takes 450+ years to break down and fragments into microplastics.

Do's

  • Rinse and compress clean bottles
  • Bag dry sachets for local plastic aggregators

Don'ts

  • Never burn plastics (releases toxic dioxins)
  • Do not drop nylon in street gutters
60M sachets used daily in Nigeria; causes severe urban drainage blockages.
Moderate

Metal

Tomato tins Peak milk cans Milo tins Drink cans

Infinitely recyclable. Highly sought after by local scrap dealers.

Do's

  • Rinse out food paste residue completely
  • Flatten cans to reduce space and sell to scrap buyers

Don'ts

  • Do not leave open tins standing outdoors (mosquito risk)
  • Avoid handling sharp jagged metal edges unshielded
Recycling aluminium cans saves 95% of energy needed for virgin smelting.
Moderate

Glass

Star/Guinness bottles Soft drink bottles Ogogoro bottles

Non-porous and durable. Circulates through deposit refund schemes.

Do's

  • Return intact bottles to retailers for deposit refund
  • Wrap broken glass in sturdy paper and label clearly

Don'ts

  • Do not mix broken window glass with container glass
  • Never throw bare loose broken glass into bags
Star and Guinness bottles are refilled an average of 25–30 times before cullet recycling.
Safe

Paper & Cardboard

Delivery cartons Newspapers Office A4

Easily pulped and remanufactured if kept clean and dry.

Do's

  • Flatten and bundle cardboard cartons flat
  • Shred clean newsprint for organic composting brown material

Don'ts

  • Do not mix grease-soaked suya wrapping paper in dry recycling
  • Never burn piles of office documents outdoors
1 tonne of recycled cardboard saves 17 trees and 26,000 litres of water.
Safe

Organic

Cassava peels Yam peels Palm kernel Food scraps

Nutrient-rich biomass suitable for composting, animal feed, or biogas.

Do's

  • Separate vegetable peels for garden compost piles
  • Dry cassava/yam peels for clean livestock feed supplementation

Don'ts

  • Do not mix plastics, batteries, or foil into compost heaps
  • Do not leave open rotting food unshielded from pests
Over 50% of Nigerian domestic waste is organic and can directly enrich agricultural soil.
Hazardous

E-Waste

Mobile phones Chargers Lanterns Inverter batteries

Contains recoverable precious metals alongside toxic heavy metals.

Do's

  • Deliver old handsets to certified take-back recyclers
  • Keep damaged lithium and lead-acid batteries stored dry

Don'ts

  • Never burn cables or circuit boards (releases lead/cadmium)
  • Never mix old batteries into general domestic rubbish
Nigeria imports and generates over 500,000 tonnes of electronics waste each year.
Safe

Textile

Ankara fabric Okrika wear Head-ties (Gele)

Natural and synthetic woven fibers suitable for mending, upcycling, or shredding.

Do's

  • Donate clean wearable clothes to charity or community drives
  • Upcycle tailoring scraps into patchwork quilts and cleaning dusters

Don'ts

  • Do not compost synthetic polyester or treated fabrics
  • Avoid discarding wearable clothes into dumpsite bins
Nigerian tailoring produces thousands of tonnes of colourful Ankara offcuts annually.
Hazardous

Hazardous

Pesticide cans Engine oil cans Paint drums

Toxic, flammable, and corrosive materials requiring professional disposal routes.

Do's

  • Keep chemicals sealed in original containers with warning labels
  • Hand used motor oil to mechanic workshops with disposal drums

Don'ts

  • Never pour agrochemicals or used oil into street gutters or drains
  • Never reuse pesticide/chemical containers for drinking water or food
1 litre of motor oil can contaminate 1 million litres of municipal fresh water aquifers.
Moderate

General / Residual

Diapers Styrofoam takeaway Mixed waste

Residual mixed items destined for municipal containment and managed landfills.

Do's

  • Sort out clean plastics, metals, and organics before bagging residue
  • Tie garbage bags securely for LAWMA or local municipal collection

Don'ts

  • Do not dump rubbish in illegal roadside piles or drainage canals
  • Do not bury mixed plastic-diaper waste in garden soil
Over 60% of dumpsite waste could be diverted through source separation.
